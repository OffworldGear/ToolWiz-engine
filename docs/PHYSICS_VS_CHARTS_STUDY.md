# Physics engine versus vendor charts: what each one does, measured

A study, not a change: no engine code, data file or existing test was touched. Everything below is regenerated by
`python3 tools/physics_vs_charts_study.py` (about 12 minutes on 4 cores, 5,572 engine runs; `--quick` takes about a minute);
the numbers are in `data/studies/physics_vs_charts.json`; `tests/test_physics_vs_charts_study.py` checks the structure.

The owner's question: charts for a named tool, physics to validate (or flag) everything else, or drop the physics and collect more
chart data? Short answer at the end of section 4. The headline: **today the answer is mostly chart-derived, but the physics
earns its place in one specific situation: the owner's Speedio with tools larger than 1/8", and long stickouts.**

## How it was measured

* 297 conformance groups (family x operation x diameter band x engagement class; square carbide end mills; at least 2 cited
  vendor rows; 2,420 rows). Each group runs on its **median tool** at the group's **median ap/D and ae/D** (the engine is
  asked for speed and feed only, as in `tests/test_vendor_conformance.py`).
* Three ways, same code, no engine edits:
  * **a, as shipped, unbranded.** Vendor guardrail C14 on (Harvey + Helical default chart, else cross-vendor consensus).
  * **b, physics only.** C14 off (`params.CONSENSUS_PATH` and `VENDOR_TABLE_PATH` set to `None` inside the worker).
    C2 and C7 stay as they are (they are fitted to vendor data).
  * **c / d, "pure mechanics".** C14 off and the fitted limits relaxed by x10 through switches that exist: c = C7 speed level
    (`Margins.speed_scale`) and C2 / C2o chip limit (`Margins.limit_scale`, `Material.c2_scale`); d = C2 / C2o only.
    There is no off switch, so x10 is "as far as the code allows without editing it"; c is then bounded by the spindle.
* Machines: `test_generic_60k` (60,000 rpm, 25 kW, runout 0.0001") and the owner's Speedio (16k BT30, 5.1 kW continuous, runout 0.0002",
  provisional holder stiffness 31.4 N/um). Aggressiveness 50 % (all modes), 0 % and 100 % (a and b).
* "Decider": a limit that is within 3 % of its value (the engine's own `binding` rule). Cross-check, "removing the chart changes the answer
  by more than 1 %", is given as "counterfactual".

## 3. Where physics changes the answer in a way a chart cannot

### 3a. Which mechanics limit decides

Physics only (b), share of groups where a mechanics limit or the machine decides the chip load or the speed:

| | Test 60k | Speedio 16k |
|---|---|---|
| mechanics (C3 / C4 / C5) decides the chip load | 14 groups, 4.7 % (C4 7, C5 4, C3 3) | **97 groups, 32.7 %** (**C5 87**, C4 7, C3 2, infeasible at the chart's depth 1) |
| spindle rpm cap decides speed | 7 (2.4 %) | 53 (17.8 %) |
| any of them | 6.7 % | **49.5 %** |
| spindle-power groups by diameter | | all 87 larger than 1/8" (hem 25, side 28, slot 17, rough 17); micro tools hit C4 / C3 instead (6 groups) |
| materials (groups where mechanics decides) | H13 7, Ni 4, 1045 2, 17-4 1 | H13 15, Ni 19, 17-4 14, 316 12, 4130 11, Ti 11, 1045 6, 6061 4, 1020 4, 420 1 |

On the test machine (25 kW, rigid) mechanics almost never speaks: **the physics adds little for common tools on a machine that has power to spare.**
On the Speedio (5.1 kW continuous, 40 N m, 16,000 rpm) it decides about a third of the groups, always for tools larger than 1/8", where the charts (written for 15-30 kW machines) ask for more
power than the spindle has.

### 3b. What following the chart blindly would do (the chart's median SFM and fz at the group's ap and ae, checked against C1/C3/C4/C5/C15)

| | Test 60k | Speedio 16k |
|---|---|---|
| chart as printed violates a mechanics limit | 15 groups, 5.1 % | **96 groups, 32.3 %** |
| the spindle cannot turn the chart's speed | 3 (1.0 %) | 50 (16.8 %) |
| either of the two | 5.7 % | **47.1 %** |
| violations by limit | C5 7, C4 4, C15 1 | C5 88, C3 7, C4 7, C15 1 |
| by diameter band | micro 3 %, 1/16-1/8" 4 %, larger 6 % | micro 9 %, 1/16-1/8" 4 %, **larger 50 %** |
| by operation | hem 10 %, slot 8 %, rough 4 %, side 2 % | hem 39 %, slot 40 %, rough 35 %, side 24 % |
| shipped feed where it is violated, over the chart's feed | median 0.37 (0.08-0.78) | median 0.18 (0.005-1.07) |

(The count also lists the chart breaking the engine's fitted C2 chip limit in 89 groups and the C7 tool-life speed in 132-143; those are the vendor-fitted limits and say only that
the charts differ from their own median fit, so they are not counted here.)

On the Speedio, the shipped answer for these groups is on average **5 times lower** than the chart (feed 0.18 x chart median), and the chart would overload the spindle by up to 15 times its limit
(C5 over its limit 9 to 15 x for 7/8"-1" tools in H13, 4130, 1045 and 17-4). That is the physics engine's real contribution, quantified: one machine in three, and one tool in two above 1/8".

|---|---|---|---|
| vendor reach (base) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0 / 0 |
| 2 | 1.00 (1.00) | 1.00 (0.99) | 0 / 0 |
| 4 | 1.00 (1.00) | 1.00 (0.93) | 0 / 2 |
| 6 | **0.84 (0.23)** | **0.90 (0.27)** | 6 / 9 |
| 8 | **0.16 (0.02)** | **0.50 (0.03)** | 11 / 11 |

| Runout (in) | Shipped MRR vs base | Shipped answers with every flute cutting, test / Speedio | Chart-only breaks, test / Speedio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0001 | 1.00 | 95 % / 95 % | 1 / 6 |
| 0.0004 | 1.00 | 55 % / 55 % | 1 / 6 |
| 0.001 | 1.00 | 45 % / 30 %; at 8xD 20 % / 5 % | 2 / 7 |

* Stickout to 4 x D costs nothing; **6 x D costs 10-16 % of the removal rate at the median and three quarters in the worst fifth; 8 x D cuts it to 16 % (test) or 50 % (Speedio)**, and the speed comes down 4 % (C9 reach factor).
  A chart gives the same speed and feed at every stickout; with 8 x D it violates deflection (C3) in 11 of 20 groups.
* Runout barely changes speed or feed (the engine keeps the chip load and only lets the flutes rub), but it decides whether all flutes cut: 95 % at 0.0001", 55 % at 0.0004", 45 %/30 % at 0.001".
  No chart records this; the engine reports it (`flutes_cutting`) but does not change the answer for it, which is a gap (section 4).
* Machine: the same 20 groups ship the same speed on both machines (the Speedio's 16k limits 2 of 20) but the Speedio's chip load is cut by C5 in 5 of 20; the chart as printed breaks spindle power in 5 of 20 and cannot be reached in 2 of 20.

### 3d. What the physics would do alone (modes c and d)

Relaxing the vendor-fitted C2 / C2o x10 (mode d, speed kept) lets the chip load rise to a median 3.0 x the physics-only value (2.0 x on the Speedio) and **2.3 x the chart's maximum chip load** (median over 297 groups);
C4 (tool bending stress) then decides 186 of 297 groups, C5 47, C15 chip room 35, C3 6, and the machine (C11) 23. Relaxing C7 as well (mode c) sends 136 groups to the 60,000 rpm cap.
So **the mechanics limits on their own are not tight enough to stand in for a chart; C2 is the limit that keeps the chip load at chart levels, and it was fitted to the charts.**
Mechanics still binds in 83-96 % of groups once the fitted limits are loose, which shows they are what stops the chip load climbing, but at values (2.3 x chart max) nobody has run.

## 4. Verdict

**Coverage first.** Cells = family x operation x diameter band (240, ten families x four operations x six bands): no chart at all in 37 (15 %), one vendor in 59 (25 %), two or more in 144 (60 %).
By operation, no chart / one vendor / two or more: slot 5 / 9 / 46; side 4 / 10 / 46; hem 11 / 19 / 30; rough 17 / 21 / 22. By band: <= 1/32" 15 / 15 / 10; 1/32-1/16" 9 / 13 / 18; 1/16-1/8" 3 / 6 / 31;
1/8-1/4" 2 / 7 / 31; 1/4-1/2" 2 / 9 / 29; > 1/2" 6 / 9 / 25. The owner's materials (Ti, 17-4, 7075 and 6061, 4130, H13; 1/32" to 1/2"; 100 cells): none 10, one vendor 18, two or more 72.
Uncovered or single-vendor cells that matter: 6061 slot, side, hem, rough at <= 1/32" and 1/32-1/16" (none or Emuge only), 6061 rough 1/16-1/8" and 1/4-1/2" (one each); 17-4 hem <= 1/16" (Garr only) and rough <= 1/32" (Harvey only);
4130 slot and side <= 1/32" and roughing <= 1/16" (none), hem <= 1/16" (Garr only), roughing 1/8-1/2" (Helical only); H13 hem and rough <= 1/16" (Garr or Harvey only); Ti hem <= 1/32" (Garr only), rough <= 1/16" (Harvey only).
Not covered by the physics engine at all: T-slot, keyseat and Woodruff cutters (`tslot_model.py`), drills and taps (hole-tool path, v1), chamfer, form and taper mills (v1). Ball, bull-nose and high-feed
end mills are covered by v2 with their own chart tables (1,466 + 1,291 + 1,019 benchmark rows against 3,376 square; those were not part of this study).

**The two options:**

* **"Physics decides, charts only validate or flag."** Not yet, and the measurement says why. With the charts off the physics lands inside the cited range for both speed and chip load in only
  **51 % of groups (43 % on the Speedio)** and above the maximum in 35 % (27 %). That is not validation, it would flag half the catalogue. And the physics is not independent of the charts:
  its two main limits (C7 speed, C2 chip) were fitted to chart medians, and without C2 the chip load goes to 2.3 x the charts' maximum. The independent part (C1, C3, C4, C5, C15, rpm and feed caps) is
  real but binds on the Speedio for 1 group in 3 and on a rigid 60k machine for 1 in 20.
* **"Drop the physics, amass more vendor data."** Also no. A chart has no spindle, stickout, runout or machine in it. On the Speedio the printed chart would overload the spindle, deflect the tool or ask for
  a speed the spindle cannot turn in **47 % of the groups** (50 % of the groups above 1/8"), and at 6 to 8 x D stickout in 7 to 12 of 20 groups. Adding data does not fix that, and 40 % of the cells still have at most one vendor.
* **What the evidence supports, the third option: charts for speed level and nominal chip load; physics for everything that depends on the actual setup.** This is what ships (C14 plus C2/C7 as chart-fitted, mechanics on top), and the numbers
  agree with it: the chart-derived answer is right in the middle of the data and the physics changes it where the setup matters (Speedio >1/8", stickout >= 6 x D, micro tools). Change the framing, not the architecture:
  call C2 and C7 "chart-fitted" in the interface, let the chart decide speed and nominal chip load when there is one, and let the mechanics limits only lower it.
  A fourth refinement the data allows: **use the physics as a flag, not as a floor, where it is above the chart.** In the 35 % of groups where physics-only is above the chart's maximum (H13 hardened, micro, nickel,
  roughing speed), the shipped guardrail is correct to cap; where the physics is below the chart (6061 chip load, 1045 chip load, C5 on the Speedio), it should win, as it does.

**What would have to be built for "physics decides" to be safe, ranked by the measured gap (the weak models):**

1. **Heat / tool-life speed against hardness (C7).** Above the chart maximum in 17.8 % of groups, 67 % of roughing groups, 34 % of micro groups; worst 4 to 9 x in hardened H13 (481 HB: 250-270 SFM against 60). Needs a hardness x coating x tool-grade speed law checked against hardened-steel data.
2. **Minimum chip and chip-load limit for micro tools and alloys (C1 / C2).** Chip load above the chart maximum in 32 % of micro groups, 37.5 % in H13, 40 % in nickel; below the minimum in 72 % of 6061 groups and 41 % of 1045 groups. The C2 fit is one number per band and material.
3. **Spindle power and torque on the real machine (C5).** The shipped Speedio limit decides 87 groups; the torque curve is "not yet checked against the Brother chart" and the holder stiffness is provisional (31.4 N/um, owner to measure). It is the most valuable and least verified model.
4. **Stickout, deflection and runout response (C3, C1).** C3 moves the answer from 6 x D; but the engine does not reduce the answer when runout stops all flutes cutting (55 % at 0.0004"). Needs a rule and test cuts.
5. **Chip evacuation (C15).** Binds only once C2 is loosened (22-35 groups); no field data to say whether it is right.
6. **Adaptive feed ceiling (C11, assumed effective feed).** Sets the chip load of 4 to 51 groups (test) and caps 6061 hem at 0.16 x the chart. An assumption, not measured.
7. **Ramp-entry end teeth, tool wear / edge-radius growth, coating-specific heat, tool-grade (cheap import vs premium) strength.** Not measured by this study; the engine has no model of the last two.

## 5. Unnamed tools, the tool grade, "Haas"

**What the engine does today for a tool with no brand.** Vendor charts come from Harvey + Helical's conservative default charts (mode "default" in 81 % of groups, cross-vendor consensus 13 %, no chart 5.7 %);
at the 50 % slider the chart value is the **middle** of that range (for 17-4 slotting 1/8": 106-140-165 SFM, so 140). The V4 notes record that this default is below the faster vendors (Sandvik, Seco, Kennametal) and that unbranded groups agree with Harvey/Helical at 1.00.
In this study the shipped unbranded answer is 0.77 x the chart's median SFM and 0.80 x its feed (median over 297 groups).

**Does the add-in's "Tool Performance Grade" change anything?** No. The same request with tool "HP Carbide Endmill" and "Carbide Endmill" gave **identical** SFM, chip load, feed, MRR and binding limits in all six groups tried
(17-4 slot 1/8", Ti slot <= 1/32", nickel slot 1/32-1/16", 316 slot 1/4-1/2", H13 slot 1/16-1/8", 1045 side > 1/2"). The grade matters only for HSS or cobalt (not carbide, so the request goes to v1, and the v2 scope rule says "carbide only").

**What "Haas" resolves to.** Nothing: `toolwiz_common.brand.resolve("Haas")` returns `None` and so does "Haas Automation"; the text is kept (`brand_text`) and reported as ignored, and the result is identical to an unbranded request. The owner says Haas tools are made by YG-1.

so a Haas = YG-1 map would trade wider evidence for brand-specific numbers and make Haas titanium noticeably lower, not higher. (That Haas tools are YG-1 products is the owner's statement; nothing in the repo confirms it.)

**"Run more conservatively, but not too much lower": evidence and suggestion, kept apart.**

*Evidence in the repo.* Nothing measures import tools. `docs/research/carbide_and_edge_radius.md` documents: transverse rupture strength of carbide grades from 2.2 to 4.6 GPa depending on the grade and the test (rod versus plate, 4-point versus bar; the engine uses 3,800 MPa and a
fatigue fraction of 0.375); cutting-edge radius 1-4 um for new micro tools, up to 20 um depending on coating (the engine uses 2 um, marked placeholder); and the C1 minimum chip is 0.2-0.4 of the edge radius. So a poorer grade or a larger edge radius are physically plausible,
but no number ties them to "cheap". The owner's steer (HANDOFF) is that cheap carbide has done better than expected.

| TRS -20 % (C4) | 1.00 (0.58) | only the 1 in 20 groups where C4 decides moves |
| edge radius x1.5 or x2 (C1) | 1.00 (0.997) | C1 never binds at these chips |
| runout 0.0004" | 1.00 (0.73 test, 0.92 Speedio) | changes which flutes cut, not the feed |
| **slider 25 % instead of 50 %** | **0.57 test / 0.60 Speedio** (worst 0.36) | SFM x0.88, chip x0.84 |
| all four together | 0.57 test / 0.60 Speedio | the slider does it |

The strength and edge-radius knobs would change almost nothing for typical groups; the only knob with a real effect is the position on the chart (the slider).

*Suggestion (not tested, interpolated from the 25 % / 50 % points above, each 10 slider points is about 15 % of MRR):* a "generic / import carbide" setting that (1) keeps the Harvey + Helical default chart and puts the unnamed default at
about **40 %** (about 15 % lower MRR than today, not 40 %); (2) uses TRS at the low end of the documented range and a larger edge radius only for tools under 1/8", where they matter; (3) leaves the user's runout alone. A premium or named brand then moves
to 50 % on its own chart. If the owner's experience is that cheap tools perform well, 50 % may stand; the data cannot say. Whether to also add Haas as a YG-1 alias is an owner decision: it raises 17-4 slotting by 30-47 % and lowers titanium by 14-31 %.

## Assumptions and what could not be isolated

* Engagement is the chart's (median ap/D and ae/D), not the engine's recommendation; so "mechanics decides" is for a chart-like cut. The recommended-engagement mode (which also uses C8, C14lo) was not run.
* The group's stickout is the vendor's (LB, else flute length + half the shank); the Speedio runout 0.0002" is assumed. A realistic setup with a longer stickout would make mechanics bind sooner (3c).
* "Within the range" uses the cited rows' min to max (nominal values included) with 1 % tolerance; the SFM range is clipped to what the spindle can turn. Ranges mix hardnesses and coatings inside a group (the H13 groups run 50-60 HRC at 60 SFM on the chart).
* C7 and C2 cannot be switched off; mode c / d scale them x10 (`Margins.speed_scale`, `limit_scale`, `Material.c2_scale`). C2o (the p90 overload chip) had to be scaled too. The vendor-fitted pieces inside C5 (kc scale, Kienzle group) and C3 (holder stiffness) cannot be separated.
* "Decider" is the limit within 3 % of its value with the smallest margin; ties (for example C2 and C14f) are reported as the chart in the shares above and as separate columns in the JSON. C11 (feed cap) is counted as machine, not mechanics.
* The tool-life speed C7 also includes the C9 reach factor (stickout); it shows up in the speed column only above 6 x D.
* Not covered by this study: ball, bull-nose and high-feed mills, finishing passes, ramp entries, the add-in's own dialog, hardness other than each group's median.
* The test machine profile (60k, 25 kW) is not a real machine; the Speedio's torque curve and holder stiffness are the repo's provisional values.
